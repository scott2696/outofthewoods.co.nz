Provably fair casino games, explained

A provably fair casino publishes a cryptographic seed before each round and the result afterwards, so you can verify that the outcome was fixed before you bet and was not altered. It applies only to a casino’s own in-house games — dice, crash, plinko — never to third-party pokies, which are certified by laboratories instead. It is a real guarantee about a narrow set of games, not a site-wide seal of honesty, and it is often sold as the latter.

Tax: what actually applies

This trips up more New Zealanders than any other aspect of crypto gambling, because there are two separate tax questions and the answers differ.

Crypto and tax for New Zealand casino players
QuestionPositionWhy
Are my casino winnings taxable?No, for a recreational playerInland Revenue does not treat casual gambling winnings as income. A person gambling as a business is the rare exception.
Is buying and selling crypto taxable?Potentially yesIRD treats cryptoassets as property. A gain on disposal is taxable if you acquired the asset with the purpose of disposing of it — which buying USDT to fund a casino arguably is.
What counts as a disposal?Converting to NZD, to another coin, or spending itNot just cashing out to your bank. Swapping USDT for BTC is a disposal.
What records should I keep?Every purchase, conversion and transfer with NZD valuesIRD expects contemporaneous records. Exchanges provide statements — download them annually.
This is general information, not tax advice. If you are moving meaningful sums, talk to a New Zealand accountant who has handled cryptoassets. Inland Revenue publishes specific guidance on cryptoassets and it is worth reading in the original.
