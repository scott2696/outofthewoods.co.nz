# Competitor research — "best online casino sites NZ"

Research conducted September 2026. Targets: the pages ranking for *best online
casino sites NZ* and its variants in New Zealand, plus the equivalent pages in
the UK, US, AU and CA markets that set the pattern NZ affiliates copy.

Sources analysed in full: gambling.com/nz/online-casinos, casinos.com/nz,
bettingtop10.co.nz/online-casinos, casino.org (US), gambling.com/uk, plus SERP
sampling for AU (PayID/pokies pages) and NZ payments/legal queries.
casino.guru/new-zealand returned HTTP 403 to automated fetch and was assessed
from SERP data only.

| Tier | Sites | Page shape |

**The winning page shape is consistent across all five countries:** a long-form
guide (6,000–8,500 words) whose first screen is a ranked toplist, followed by
methodology, then segmented "best for X" lists, then education (games, payments,
law, bonuses), then FAQ. Nobody in the top 10 ranks with a short page.

## 2. What top competitors do well

### Content strategy and topical coverage
- **casinos.com/nz** is the most complete: 12 H2 sections covering toplist,
  segmented recommendations, bonuses, games, NZ law, methodology, sign-up,
  payments, cash-out tips, safety, news, guides, FAQ. ~6,500–8,000 words.

  Act explainer with implementation dates, DIA licensing structure, the 15-licence

  high payouts", and game-specific picks (blackjack, roulette, live).
- **casino.org** operates an *ecosystem* rather than a page: news, free games,

- Interactive toplist carousels with expandable operator cards (gambling.com).
- Comparison matrices with rating, bonus, RTP, min deposit, payout window,
  payment icons (bettingtop10 runs a 10-row matrix with average RTP per casino).
- Payment-method icon rows inline on every operator card — fast visual scanning.
- Anchor-link jump menus at the top of the page.
- casino.org: blacklist, big-wins tracker, real-time game data, free-play library.
- Sidebar sticky CTAs and bottom mobile nav bars.

### UX and conversion elements used repeatedly
- Rank number + logo + bonus headline + "Play Now" button, repeated 10–20 times.
- Dual CTA per operator: primary affiliate button + secondary "Read Review".
- "18+, New Customers Only, T&Cs apply" under every listing (compliance + trust).
- Disclosure box near the top (affiliate transparency).
- Last-updated timestamp near the byline — casinos.com goes to the minute.
- Progressive disclosure: collapsed FAQ accordions, expandable operator detail.

### EEAT implementation
This is where the good competitors are genuinely strong, and it is the bar to clear:

## 3. Weaknesses and missed opportunities — our attack surface

These are ranked by how exploitable they are.

### A. Payment information is materially out of date (highest-value gap)
**POLi is still shown as a casino deposit method on ranking pages** — including
gambling.com's operator cards. POLi the company is alive (acquired by Merco,
bank-API connected since the December 2025 open-banking rules, eight NZ bank
partners) but **it is no longer available at NZ online casinos in 2026**.

Any page still showing a POLi logo on a casino card was built from a template
and never re-checked. This is a verifiable, checkable error on high-ranking
pages, and correcting it publicly is a trust signal competitors cannot copy
without admitting they were wrong.

**Our response:** a payments page and a payments table on every relevant page
that states the position plainly, with a callout naming the error.

### B. No tabulated payout data — claims without measurement
Every competitor *claims* fast payouts. Nobody publishes a dataset. casinos.com
evaluates "banking and processing" as a criterion but tabulates nothing.
bettingtop10 claims real-money withdrawal testing but publishes no medians or
sample sizes.

**Our response:** a timed-withdrawal programme with published medians, fastest,
slowest, sample size (n) and hold counts per operator, refreshed monthly. Sample
sizes published deliberately — a median with no n is marketing.

### C. No RTP configuration auditing
Everyone quotes RTP as if it were fixed. In reality studios ship the same title
at 96.5% / 94.5% / 92% and the *operator chooses*. bettingtop10 tabulates
"average RTP" per casino with no stated source. Nobody explains configuration.

**Our response:** a 10-title RTP sample per casino, read from in-game paytables,
published as "X of 10 at studio default" with the affected titles named. This is
genuinely novel in the NZ market.

### D. Bonus comparison is on headline size, not clearable value
Universal weakness. Pages rank "600% up to $19,500" above "100% at 10x" because
the number is bigger. Nobody publishes turnover-required arithmetic.

**Our response:** a turnover table showing what a standard NZ$100 deposit
actually costs in required turnover and expected cost to clear, across every
offer. This reframes the entire category on a metric we define.

### E. Live dealer table counts are unverified
Competitors quote "500+ live tables" from operator marketing. Nobody counts, and
nobody accounts for the timezone problem — 9am NZST is 10pm in Riga, and a thin
white-label floor is invisible if you check at the wrong hour.

**Our response:** manual table counts from an NZ IP at a stated hour, broken
down by game type, with 24h auto-table availability flagged.

### F. Weekly withdrawal caps are almost never discussed
casinos.com mentions "withdrawal caps disclosed" as a criterion. No competitor
tabulates cap against maximum advertised bonus — which is where the trap is.

**Our response:** a cap-versus-bonus table and a "weeks to extract the maximum"
column.

### G. Thin or missing sections competitors leave open
| Gap | Who is missing it |
|---|---|
| Support response-time benchmarks (quantified) | All |
| Withdrawal-dispute escalation, step by step | All |
| Deposit/withdrawal fee and FX-spread comparison | All |
| "Casinos we rejected and why" transparency | All except casino.org's blacklist |
| Bonus code / promo tracker | All NZ competitors |
| VIP tier-by-tier comparison | All |
| Mobile app vs browser, measured | All (bettingtop10 mentions handsets only) |
| NZ greyhound racing closure (1 Aug 2026) | All betting pages still list NZ greyhounds |
| Post-Dec-2026 transition guidance for players' balances | All |
| Affiliate-marketing ban in the 2026 regs (self-disclosure) | All — nobody admits it |

### H. EEAT is claimed but rarely evidenced
Competitors name authors and state methodologies, but the *outputs* are not
traceable to the process. "We time withdrawals" is asserted; no timings are
shown. "25-step review process" is a marketing line with no steps published.

**Our response:** publish the weights, the steps, the sample sizes, the
disqualification criteria, and — critically — the commission rates versus the
rankings, so independence is demonstrable rather than asserted.

### I. Schema markup is weak
No competitor page inspected showed FAQPage, Review or ItemList schema in the
served HTML. bettingtop10 was flagged in our audit as a "quick win" opportunity
for exactly this reason. The SERP feature real estate is unclaimed.

## 4. Content patterns to adopt (what works, keep it)

1. **Toplist above the fold**, rank + logo + bonus + score + dual CTA.
2. **Segmented "best for" grid** — captures long-tail intent on one page.
3. **Anchor-link TOC** — earns sitelinks and improves dwell.
4. **Named author + named fact-checker + last-updated + next-review date.**
5. **Methodology link in the first screen**, not buried.
6. **FAQ accordion of 8–15 questions** using verbatim PAA phrasing.
7. **Disclosure box near the top**, not only in the footer.
8. **Local RG resources with phone numbers**, on every page.
9. **Deep internal silo**: payments, games, bonuses, operators, law.
10. **Monthly refresh with a visible change log** — casinos.com and bettingtop10
    both run "recent updates" blocks and it clearly works for freshness.

## 5. Conversion elements seen repeatedly

- "Play Now" is the dominant primary CTA. Secondary is "Read Review".
- No countdown timers or false urgency on the sites that rank — compliance risk.
- Payment icons inline on cards act as a pre-qualification filter.
- Score out of 10 next to the CTA (not out of 5) — finer granularity reads as
  more analytical.
- Sticky CTA on mobile; bottom nav bar on NZ-local sites.
- Bonus terms in small text directly under the offer (compliance + trust).

## 6. SERP targeting observed

Competitors are targeting, and largely failing to capture:
- **Featured snippet** for "best online casino NZ" — currently a list snippet.
- **People Also Ask**: legality, tax, safety, NZD, fastest payout, minimum age.
- **FAQ rich results** — largely unclaimed because schema is missing.
- **Review stars** — unclaimed on the category pages.
- **Sitelinks** — earned only by the largest domains.
- **"2026" / month freshness in titles** — universal, and now table stakes.

## 7. Summary: how we win

| Competitor strength | Our counter |
|---|---|
| Huge word counts | Match the depth, but every section carries a number they cannot cite |
| Named authors | Five named editors with declared beats, individual profile pages, Person schema |
| Claimed methodology | Published weights, published steps, published disqualification criteria, published commission-vs-ranking evidence |
| Claimed testing | Published datasets with sample sizes: 241 withdrawals, RTP samples, table counts, margin measurements |
| NZ law coverage (bettingtop10 only) | Deeper: Act + Regulations + fees + auction format + penalties + levy rates + what it means for players, written from primary sources |
| Payment sections | Corrected: POLi removed, with the correction stated as a trust signal |
| Bonus comparison | Reframed onto turnover-required, with worked arithmetic |
| No schema | Full @graph: Organization, WebSite, Article, BreadcrumbList, ItemList, Product/Review, FAQPage, Person, ProfilePage |
| Recommends everything | Lists operators it advises caution about, with reasons and a low trust score |

The strategic bet: **competitors compete on comprehensiveness; we compete on
verifiability.** Every differentiator above is a number with a method behind it,
which is both a ranking asset (unique content, entity depth, EEAT) and a
conversion asset (readers trust numbers with sample sizes).
